title: WP Customers
description: How a water purveyor manages the people its automated email is addressed to — the customers screen, its procedures, and the row policies behind them
---

Customers are the occupants and billing contacts an asset's automated email goes
to. They never sign in: there is no customer role, no customer login, and no
self-service path to any of this. Every record is created and maintained by the
water purveyor that owns it, from `/wp/customers`.

This page describes the screen as built. It covers *WP dashboard — customers* in
the [build plan](/docs/build-plan) and PRD § 6.6.4.

## What a customer record is

| Field | Required | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `name` | yes | Trimmed; a name of only spaces is empty, not valid |
| `email` | yes | Lowercased and trimmed, so one address has one spelling. Unique **within the purveyor** — see below |
| BIN (`attachBin`) | yes, while the customer holds no asset | Not a column — the BIN of an asset to attach the customer to on save. See [The BIN](#the-bin) |
| `account_number` | no | Blank is stored as NULL, never as `''` |
| `address` | yes | Trimmed; required on every save (WP/Admin feedback). The column stays nullable, so a row saved before the rule still reads — editing it means filling it in |
| `rate_category` | yes | `standard` or `agricultural`; agricultural gets the purple badge |
| `ag_renewal_date` | agricultural only | Required on the agricultural rate, refused on the standard one |
| `status` | yes | `active` or `suspended` — see below |
| `revision` | — | Server-owned version of the row. Returned with every customer and sent back as `expectedRevision` on save — see below |
| `water_purveyor_id` | — | Never sent by the client; derived from the caller |

The record attaches to the *customer*, not to an asset. Which assets a customer
currently holds lives in `customer_assignments`, with `bfa_assets.current_customer_id`
as the live pointer — occupants change over time, and that history is not this
screen's to rewrite. The one thing this screen does to it is **attach**: the BIN
field opens a new occupancy exactly as the asset page's *Attach customer* does.
It never closes one on its own or detaches an asset; that stays on the asset's
page, next to the history.

### The BIN

Every automated notice is addressed through an asset's current customer
([PRD § 9](/docs/prd#9-confirmed-decisions-client-qa-log)), so a customer who
occupies no asset is a record no notice can reach. The client asked for the BIN
on this form for exactly that reason, and the rule is: **after a save, the
customer holds at least one asset.**

- **Add customer** asks for a BIN, required. The customer is inserted and
  attached in one transaction, so a BIN that cannot be honoured leaves no
  customer behind.
- **Edit customer** lists the BINs the customer already holds (*Attached
  BINs*, read from `bfa_assets.current_customer_id` within the purveyor) and
  offers *Attach another BIN*, optional. A customer who holds none — a row saved
  before the rule — sees a required *BIN* field instead.
- The table has a **BIN** column; a customer holding nothing shows a *No BIN*
  warning badge rather than a dash.

Saving a BIN runs `attachCustomerToAsset` (`packages/api/src/lib/customer-assignment.ts`),
the same three writes `assets.assignCustomer` makes — close the open occupancy
row, open one from today, move the live pointer — and, for an active customer,
resumes a held notice chain. The match is exact on the trimmed BIN, inside the
caller's purveyor, under a row lock on the asset. Attaching does not change the
customer row, so it neither needs nor bumps `revision`.

What a BIN can be refused for, and where the form shows it:

| Case | Code | Shown |
|---|---|---|
| No BIN, and the customer holds none | `BAD_REQUEST` | Under the BIN field |
| No asset in this purveyor has the BIN (another purveyor's included) | `NOT_FOUND` | Under the BIN field |
| The asset is held by **another** customer | `PRECONDITION_FAILED` | Under the BIN field, naming the occupant, with **Move it to this customer** |

Moving an asset away from another occupant redirects its automated email, so it
waits for that button, which resends the save with `acknowledgeReassign`. The
previous occupancy is closed with today's date, not erased. A BIN the customer
already holds is a no-op. A *suspended* customer is not asked again — the form
states what suspension does as soon as it is chosen.

The messages live in `packages/api/src/lib/customer-bin.ts`, import-free, so the
form can route them without pulling the router into the bundle. Every attachment
writes the same `bfa_asset_customer_assigned` audit line as the asset page.

### Why the AG renewal date is conditional

The AG renewal notice ([PRD § 6.9](/docs/prd)) fires against `ag_renewal_date`.
An agricultural customer without one is a notice that can never be scheduled, so
the date is required at the point the rate is chosen rather than left for the
workflow to discover.

Saving the customer is what schedules it. Creating an agricultural customer
queues one renewal email to the customer's own address for midnight Hawaii time
on that date; changing the date moves it; moving the customer to the standard
rate cancels it. Once the email is delivered to an active customer, the date
moves on its own to one calendar year after the Hawaii day of that delivery
(29 February → 28 February in a non-leap year) and next year's email is queued;
a suspended customer is not mailed and keeps their date. See
[Workflow 2](/docs/notice-workflow#workflow-2--the-ag-rate-renewal-notice). The form only shows the field on the agricultural rate,
and only that rate asks for it: the date is typed as `MM/DD/YYYY` or chosen from the calendar picker, must be a real
calendar day, and is sent as the API's `YYYY-MM-DD`.

### The revision check

The edit dialog submits the **whole record**, which used to mean the record as
it looked when the form was opened. That became a problem once the AG renewal
notice started moving `ag_renewal_date` forward by itself: a clerk who opened
the form before the nightly transition and saved it afterwards sent the old
date back, which read as a deliberate date change — the fresh successor was
cancelled and a job was queued for a day that had already passed, mailing the
customer a second time.

So `customers.revision` (`0020`) is an optimistic lock:

- every customer the API returns carries its `revision`;
- `updateCustomer` requires `expectedRevision` — the version the form (or the
  row's Suspend / Reactivate button) was rendered from;
- a real change is written only while that version still matches, and the same
  statement bumps it. The scheduler's annual transition bumps it too, in its own
  transaction, so the case above is exactly the case this refuses;
- a save that changes **nothing** stays a true no-op even from an out-of-date
  form: there is no edit to lose, so it writes nothing, logs nothing, and comes
  back without a conflict.

A stale save is refused with `CONFLICT` and its own sentence — *This customer
was changed after you opened this form…* — which the dialog shows as a
**form-wide** message, not under Email. Duplicate-address conflicts still go to
the Email field; the two are told apart by the message rather than by the code.
Nothing is resent automatically, the dialog stays open with what was typed, and
the list behind it is refetched so reopening the customer starts from what is
actually stored.

Name, email and address are required inline, the email must look like an address
(*Please enter a valid email address.*), and every text field reports the
server's length limit at the field — see the shared
[inline validation](/docs/design-system#form-validation) pattern. A server
refusal is shown as a form-level message inside the dialog, not a toast.

**Rate category and status open unanswered.** A new customer shows *Select…* in
both, and saving without a choice puts *Select a rate category.* / *Select a
status.* under the select instead of quietly saving *Standard* and *Active*.
Editing an existing customer keeps both prefilled with what is stored. The
form's values allow `""` only while unanswered; the payload is built from a
completed record, so `""` never reaches the procedures, whose enum validation is
unchanged.

### One email address per purveyor

Two customers in the same purveyor may not share an email address; the same
address in two different purveyors is fine, since neither can see the other's
list. `0011_tenant_email_uniqueness` enforces it with a unique index on
`(water_purveyor_id, lower(btrim(email)))` — case- and whitespace-insensitive,
matching how the app already stores addresses, so no second spelling can slip
past. Being a database constraint rather than a "does it exist yet?" check, two
concurrent saves of the same address cannot both succeed, whether they come
through the procedures or PostgREST.

`createCustomer` and `updateCustomer` catch that one unique violation (by index
name) and answer `CONFLICT` with *Another customer in this water purveyor already
uses this email address.* The form shows it under the Email field with the shared
inline error — never a toast — and clears it once the address changes. Saving a
customer without changing their own email is unaffected: a row never collides
with itself. The migration does not merge or delete anything: if a purveyor
already held duplicate addresses it stops, before creating the index, and says
so.

Going the other way clears it. Moving a customer back to the standard rate
writes NULL, in the form *and* in the procedure — a stale date left on the row
is a notice that fires for a customer who is no longer on that rate. A request
that sends a renewal date alongside the standard rate is refused outright rather
than having the date quietly dropped, so a client cannot believe it stored one.

### What suspension actually does

Suspending a customer pauses all automated email for the assets currently
assigned to them. Per the [confirmed decision](/docs/prd#9-confirmed-decisions-client-qa-log),
this is not a per-email skip: the asset's whole notice pipeline holds until the
WP attaches a different customer to the BIN.

That consequence reaches past this table, so the screen says so in three places
rather than leaving "suspended" to be interpreted:

- the status field in the form warns as soon as *Suspended* is selected,
- the **Suspend** button asks for confirmation and repeats the sentence,
- a suspended row carries *Automated email paused* under its status badge.

Reactivating goes straight through with no confirmation — restoring email is not
the destructive direction. Nothing is deleted either way, and assignments are
untouched, so the switch is reversible from the same button.

This screen sets the flag; the hold itself is enforced at send time by
[Workflow 1](/docs/notice-workflow#hold-and-resume). The notice handler resolves
the asset's current customer when the job runs, and a suspended one means it
sends nothing **and does not advance the escalation** — so nothing later in the
chain is consumed by a notice that was never delivered.

Two things end a hold, and they are not equally quick. Attaching a different
**active** customer to the BIN resumes the waiting stage immediately, in the same
transaction as the assignment. Reactivating *this* customer from this screen is
picked up by the held job's own recheck, which runs a day out — the hold is not a
retry, and there is nothing to retry until a person acts, so it is deliberately
coarse. Either way the stage that resumes is the one that was waiting; nothing
already sent is replayed.

## Procedures

`customers.*` in `packages/api/src/routers/customers.ts`. All three sit behind
`wpProcedure`.

| Procedure | Does |
|---|---|
| `listCustomers` | Every customer in the caller's purveyor, A–Z |
| `createCustomer` | One new customer, filed under the caller's purveyor |
| `updateCustomer` | Rewrites one record — every field is sent, so an omitted optional field is one being cleared |

Status changes go through `updateCustomer` rather than a fourth procedure: the
form and the suspend button are the same write, and one write means one place
where the agricultural rule is checked.

### A save that changes nothing writes nothing

The whole record is sent on every save, so a request that edits a field and one
that opens the dialog and presses save look identical on the wire.
`updateCustomer` compares the incoming values against the row it already read
and, when every writable column matches, returns that row without touching the
table.

Writing anyway would make two records lie. `updated_at` would come to mean "when
somebody last pressed save" rather than when the row last changed, and the audit
trail would carry a `customer_updated` line for a modification that never
happened — the one thing an audit trail must not do.

The comparison is driven off `customerColumns`' own keys, so a column added to
the write set is compared here too. It is an identity check on strings, enums
and nulls; clearing an optional field to `null` is a real change and is written.

### `wpProcedure`

A new procedure base next to `superAdminProcedure`, in `packages/api/src/index.ts`.
It enforces two things:

- the caller holds one of `wp_admin`, `wp_sub_admin`, `wp_employee` — a
  Super-Admin or a certifier is refused rather than quietly handed an empty
  list;
- the caller's profile carries a `water_purveyor_id`. A WP role without one is a
  broken row, and since every query scopes by that id, letting it through would
  turn a data problem into an unscoped read.

It puts the id back on the context narrowed to `string`, so downstream
procedures cannot forget the null case — by then there isn't one.

### Tenant scoping

The tenant is never an input. There is no `waterPurveyorId` field on any
customer procedure's schema, so a client has nowhere to ask for somebody else's
— and Zod strips it if one is sent anyway.

Reads and writes both carry the caller's purveyor in the query predicate.
`updateCustomer` matches on **both** `customers.id` and `water_purveyor_id`, for
the read that precedes the write as well as the write itself. A customer id from
another tenant therefore matches no row and comes back as a not-found: it cannot
be read, and it cannot be modified.

That matters because these procedures run through the privileged Drizzle
connection, which bypasses RLS. The procedure layer is the application's
authorization boundary; the policies below are a second, independent one.

## Row policies

`0003_customer_policies` adds the `customers` policies. RLS was enabled on the
table in the first migration; until now it had no policies at all, so nothing
reaching the table through PostgREST could see a row.

| Policy | Who | What |
|---|---|---|
| `customers_all_super_admin` | `super_admin` | Read, insert and update, in every tenant |
| `customers_select_own_wp` | all three WP roles | Read rows where `water_purveyor_id` matches their own |
| `customers_insert_own_wp` | all three WP roles | Insert rows into their own purveyor |
| `customers_update_own_wp` | all three WP roles | Update rows in their own purveyor, and only into it |

Caller resolution goes through the `private.current_global_role()` and
`private.current_wp_id()` helpers from `0001`, so a blocked profile resolves to
NULL and keeps no access at all — same as everywhere else.

Three deliberate omissions:

- **No DELETE for anybody, Super-Admin included.** A customer is referenced by
  `bfa_assets.current_customer_id`, by `customer_assignments` occupancy history
  and by `scheduled_jobs`; deleting one would take past occupancy with it.
  Suspension is the reversible switch that exists instead.

  The Super-Admin policy is written `FOR ALL` and still cannot delete: the
  single `GRANT SELECT, INSERT, UPDATE` is what withholds the privilege, and no
  policy can hand back one that was never granted. A Postgres policy takes one
  command or all of them, so `FOR ALL` states the policy's own scope — the
  effective access is that intersected with the grant. `customers-rls.test.ts`
  asserts the refusal rather than trusting the reading.
- **`WITH CHECK` on the update policy as well as `USING`.** They answer different
  questions — which rows can be reached, and what they may be turned into.
  Without the second, a WP could hand its own customer to another purveyor by
  writing a different `water_purveyor_id`.
- **No certifier read path yet.** The build plan describes one — customers
  reachable through `bfa_assets.current_customer_id` for an authorized purveyor,
  for wizard prefill. It belongs with the wizard; granting it now would open the
  table to a role with no screen for it.

## Audit logging

Every write emits one structured JSON line, the same shape as `caller_resolved`
and `water_purveyor_status_changed`:

| Event | When |
|---|---|
| `customer_created` | A record is created |
| `customer_updated` | A record actually changed — never for a save that changed nothing |
| `customer_status_changed` | …and the status was part of what moved, carrying `from` and `to` |

Each carries `actor_id`, `customer_id`, `wp_id` and `timestamp`. The status
change gets its own line rather than hiding inside the generic update, because
it is the field whose effect leaves this table.

## The screen

`/wp/customers`, in the standard WP shell. The table shows name, BIN(s), account number,
email, rate category, status, and per-row **Edit** and **Suspend** /
**Reactivate** actions. Create and edit are the same dialog form — the only
differences are the starting values and the button label.

Design notes, from the [design system](/docs/design-system): agricultural uses
the purple tint pair, statuses use the success and warning tint pairs, and the
page title is the only Sora on the screen. Colours come from the tokens in
`packages/ui/src/styles/globals.css`, not from hex values in the components.

The screen also introduced `Select` to `packages/ui` — the popup twin of
`Input`, built on Base UI and styled from the same tokens, because a native
`<select>` cannot carry the approved control and menu surfaces.

The two database suites seed two purveyors with deliberately interchangeable
customers — same name, same email — so a leak between them looks like a leak
rather than like a plausible row.
