Prepared for Nathan (Backflow Certifier, Hawaii) — prospect and product owner. Ground-up custom application (Next.js + Supabase), replacing a prior Bubble-based scope. Effort estimate: ~170 hours (~6 weeks, AI-assisted development).

## 1. Overview

A multi-tenant SaaS application, built natively, that replaces a manual paper/mail workflow end-to-end. Certifiers submit test results from their phone in the field, reminder and failure notices go out automatically by email, and each water company gets a dashboard showing incoming and delinquent tests in one place.

- **Who pays:** the water companies (Water Purveyors, WPs). Nathan and his certifiers use the app for free. The Water Purveyor dashboard is the core commercial product.
- **Access model:** invite only. No public signup. Nathan invites WPs, WPs invite their certifiers, and a certifier can work for more than one WP.

**What's different in this version**

- Ground-up build in code (Next.js on Vercel) instead of extending the existing Bubble app.
- Supabase (Postgres) replaces Bubble's native database, with Postgres Row-Level Security (RLS) enforcing multi-tenant isolation in place of Bubble's privacy rules.
- No data migration required — clean slate, no legacy records to carry over (client-confirmed).
- Development is AI-assisted (an agent writes implementation code under engineering supervision) — more time on architecture, review, and QA, less on reverse-engineering someone else's build.

**Assumption:** Next.js + Supabase (backend/auth) + Vercel (hosting) is the assumed/confirmed stack — the standard, best-supported combination for this profile.

## 2. Goals and Success Criteria

**Primary goals**

- Certifier submits a completed test from a phone in the field, across all 8 BFA assembly types, replacing paper and manual PDF entry.
- Annual reminders, escalating late notices, and failure notices go out automatically by email.
- Each Water Purveyor gets a single dashboard showing incoming reports, delinquent tests, key counts, and compliance trends — scoped to only their own data.
- Nathan gets a super-admin view across all WP tenants, an invite flow to onboard new WPs, and the ability to Impersonate a WP or certifier.

**What success looks like for v1**

- Nathan can demo a working end-to-end flow: certifier submits → notice fires → WP sees it on the dashboard.
- A WP can log in, see who is delinquent and what is coming in, and download their records over a chosen date range.
- Build lands at roughly 120–125 engineering hours, positioned as a real, sellable MVP.

## 4. Technology and Architecture

- **Frontend:** Next.js (React), deployed on Vercel (client-confirmed hosting).
- **Backend / data:** Supabase — Postgres, Supabase Auth (email/password), Row-Level Security for multi-tenant isolation, Supabase Storage for photo uploads and generated PDFs.
- **Transactional email:** Resend or Postmark, wired into the notice/reminder workflows.
- **Development approach:** AI-assisted — an agent handles the bulk of implementation coding, with engineering oversight for architecture, review, and QA.

**Multi-tenant isolation:** each WP sees only their own data, enforced with Postgres RLS policies keyed off the authenticated user's WP association. This must be correctly designed once, up front — RLS bugs are a security issue, not just a display bug.

**Authentication (confirmed):** Email/password. Invited users set their own password and complete their profile on first login, arriving via a unique invite link — no public signup.

## 5. Data Model (Supabase / Postgres)

The asset-centric principle (Nathan's core idea): the BIN and test history stay attached to the physical asset and meter location, not the customer, because occupants change over time. Only the meter location and its BIN are permanent. The `annual_test_date` field on `bfa_assets` is the single most important field in the schema — it drives the reminder engine (Section 6.7).

**Asset ↔ customer relationship (confirmed):** asset and customer are equally important — a wizard submission can correct any field except the BIN, which is the one truly protected value. Over time, one asset can have several customers attached in sequence (occupant changes), and one customer can be attached to several assets. `bfa_assets.current_customer_id` is a live pointer, not a fixed relationship — model the historical assignments too (a `customer_assignments` join table with a start/end date, or equivalent), not just the current pointer, so past occupancy isn't lost when a customer is swapped.

**Email routing rule (confirmed):** every automated email (reminder, late notices, failure notice, AG renewal) is sent to whichever customer is *currently* attached to the asset's BIN via `current_customer_id`. If that customer is suspended (`customer_status`), no email goes out for that asset until the WP attaches a new customer to the BIN — suspension is not a per-email skip, it's a hold on the whole asset's notice pipeline.

| Table | Purpose / key fields |
|---|---|
| `bfa_assets` | The physical valve — permanent record. BIN (protected, not correctable via a test-submission edit), serial number, make, model, size, bfa_type, service address, assembly location, annual_test_date, asset_status, water_purveyor_id, current_customer_id. |
| `customers` | Occupant/billing entity, potentially one of several attached to an asset over time. account_number, name, address, email, rate_category (e.g. Agricultural), ag_renewal_date, customer_status, water_purveyor_id. |
| `customer_assignments` | History of which customer was attached to which asset and when — needed because occupants change and both past and current assignments matter. bfa_asset_id, customer_id, start_date, end_date (null = current). |
| `certifiers` | The field tester. name, company, id_number, license info, expiration_date, status (active/blocked), linked user_id. |
| `water_purveyors` | The paying municipal client. company, address, PWSID, subdomain/branding, notice email templates, notification lead time (fixed platform-wide), approved testers list. |
| `wp_certifier_authorizations` | Join table linking a certifier to the WPs they're authorized to work for. authorization_status. Drives the WP selector dropdown. |
| `repair_companies` | Approved repair firms per WP. company, phone, email, status. Feeds the failure notice email. |
| `test_submissions` | The test record. Status (draft/submitted), values per BFA type, linked bfa_asset_id and certifier_id. Editable by the certifier only while in Draft; locked after submit for the certifier, but a WP can correct any field of a submitted test (Section 6.6.6). Draft state saves at every conditional step, not just the top level. Fields that were pre-filled from a prior submission (existing-BIN flow) and then overwritten by the certifier need an `overridden_fields` marker (or equivalent) so the WP can see what changed, not just the final value. |
| `users` / `profiles` | Supabase Auth user plus profile table. name, global_role (SuperAdmin, WPAdmin, WPsubAdmin, WPEmployee, Certifier), status (active/blocked). |
| `invitations` | Invite token log. email, role, status, token, expires_at, associated_wp_id, is_redeemed. |

**Confirmed:** an existing certifier invited by a second WP gets a normal invite email and skips profile setup on redemption — just a new `wp_certifier_authorizations` row.

**Three tables beyond this list, per the [Build Plan](/docs/build-plan#table-creation-order):** `notice_templates` (one row per WP per notice type — a missing row falls back to platform-default text, not a blank send), `wp_feature_flags` (automated reminders and the AG renewal notice turned out to be **optional, paid add-ons** the Super-Admin switches on per WP — not something every WP gets by default; a missing row means off), and `scheduled_jobs` (not a PRD line item on its own, but required since Vercel Cron is off the table — see the portability constraints). The Build Plan is the authoritative schema going forward; this section stays as the original PRD-level model.

## 7. Key User Flows

1. **Test submission (existing asset)** — certifier enters BIN, specs pre-populate, runs test, submits; record locks, PDF generates; result lands on the WP dashboard.
2. **Test submission (new install, no BIN)** — same wizard, certifier-entered fields; WP reviews and assigns a BIN in BFA Assets, which is also where the Annual Test Date gets set (puts the asset on the reminder schedule).
3. **Direct BFA Asset registration by the WP** — WP registers an asset directly from the dashboard, independent of any certifier submission.
4. **Notice escalation** — reminder ~2 months ahead → Late Notice 1 (+1 day) → Late Notice 2 (+31 days) → Final Notice (+31 days). A passing test stops the escalation.
5. **Failure to repair** — failed test triggers an immediate failure email; asset appears on the WP fail list after 30 days without a passing re-test; clears on the next pass.
6. **Invitation onboarding** — inviter sends invite → invited person sets password + completes profile on first login → an already-registered certifier authorized for an additional WP skips repeating profile setup.
7. **Correcting a mistaken submission** — two paths, both confirmed in scope: (a) the certifier re-runs the wizard for the same BIN and the new submission replaces the prior answers (existing-BIN prefill, any field overwritable); or (b) the certifier flags it to the WP, who opens the submission's document view (Section 6.6.6) and corrects any field directly — including cases where the certifier left BIN blank and the WP needs to work out which asset it belongs to.

## 8. Non-Functional Requirements

- **Multi-tenant isolation:** each WP sees only their own data, enforced by Postgres RLS. Verified by explicit cross-tenant QA testing.
- **Invite-only access:** no public signup for the app; only the landing-page lead-capture form is public.
- **Data immutability:** a certifier can edit a test only while it's a Draft; locked after submit. A WP can correct any field of a submitted test (confirmed — full field-level edit); a certifier's only path to change a submitted test is re-running the wizard for the same BIN, which overwrites the prior answers. The one field neither path treats casually is the BIN itself — it's the most protected value in a submission.
- **Mobile-first certifier experience:** wizard and dashboard must work well on a phone. WP and Super-Admin dashboards are desktop-oriented.
- **No payment handling:** the app processes no money; WP billing happens outside the app by invoice.

## 10. Open Questions for the Client

Four — the [Build Plan](/docs/build-plan#open-questions-to-close-before-the-owning-feature-ships) resolved most of the original list, and building § 6.6.3 and § 6.7 each surfaced one that had not been asked:

- **WPSubAdmin vs. WPEmployee permission split.** All three WP roles can invite certifiers and view submitted tests (confirmed above), but the general difference *between* SubAdmin and Employee is still undefined, and everywhere it has not been asked the two share identical policies. Two screens have now decided it for themselves, both narrowly:
  - § 6.6.3 — `certifiers.status`: all three roles list and invite, only Admin and SubAdmin block ([WP Certifiers](/docs/wp-certifiers)).
  - § 6.6.6 — submitted tests: all three roles read the list and open the document, only Admin and SubAdmin correct one or link it to an asset ([WP Submitted Tests](/docs/wp-submitted-tests)). This is the specific question this section used to name — *can an Employee edit a submitted test, or only view it* — and the screen shipped on "only view", because correcting one rewrites the legal record of a backflow test and widening a permission later is reversible in a way that narrowing it is not.

  Neither settles anything beyond its own screen. Both checks live in their own router rather than a shared procedure precisely so the next feature cannot inherit one by accident, and the client's general answer is still wanted.
- **Is blocking a certifier global or per-WP?** § 6.6.3 shipped with the global reading, because that is what the schema comment (`blocked prevents login`), the build plan's acceptance criterion ("blocking a certifier prevents their login") and this section's own data model ("update `status` only") all describe. The consequence is worth confirming rather than inheriting: `certifiers.status` is one column on one row, so a purveyor blocking a certifier who also works for two others removes that person's platform access entirely, and those purveyors are neither consulted nor told — on a platform whose premise is that a certifier can work for more than one WP. The narrower alternative already exists in the schema (`wp_certifier_authorizations.authorization_status = 'revoked'`) and would end one relationship while leaving the account alone. The two are different products, and Workflow 1 already reads both columns as different things. The five things that change if the answer is "per-WP" are listed in [WP Certifiers](/docs/wp-certifiers#if-that-is-wrong-here-is-what-changes).
- **Full list of dynamic variables** for customizable notice email text, beyond customer name / BFA type / BIN / service address / due date. The client has offered to pull the full list from the old Bubble database.
- **Where a held notice pipeline resumes.** § 9 confirms that a suspended customer stops an asset's automated email until a new customer is attached to the BIN, and says nothing about what the new occupant then receives. § 6.7 shipped on the narrowest reading — **the single unsent stage resumes, and nothing already sent is replayed** — so a customer attached three months into an escalation gets the stage that was waiting rather than the reminder and two late notices the previous occupant's suspension swallowed. The two alternatives are restarting from the reminder, which writes to a new occupant about a deadline that has already passed, and skipping to whichever stage the calendar now says is due, which can take a customer from silence straight to a final notice. Each is a change to one function; the reasoning is in [Notice Workflow & Scheduler](/docs/notice-workflow#the-resume-point-stated).

## 12. Deferred — Out of Scope for v1

- Automated monthly statements (midnight cron, white-labeled PDF, auto-email to WP admins) — heavier than the basic on-demand CSV export included above.
- Master audit ledger for Super-Admin actions.
- Certifier-level analytics/charts (WP and Super-Admin get analytics; certifiers do not, for v1).
- Help center, quick-start video, or in-app AI copilot.
- Interactive demo sandbox with a role-picker on the landing page.
- Bulk CSV/XLSX import wizard for onboarding a new WP's historical data (not needed — clean-slate build).
- In-app payment or checkout — WPs are invoiced separately outside the app.

## 13. Team Notes

The wizard (Section 6.5) is the single largest and most detail-sensitive module — 8 distinct BFA types with real conditional branching, and several open questions concentrate there; resolving those early reduces rework risk more than any other single action.
